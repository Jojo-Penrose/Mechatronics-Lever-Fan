What this shows is that the initial response follows a second-order system, but
the sticktion kicks in when the velocity drops. So, this is a pretty bad second-
order approximation actually. The good news is that PID tuning really doesn't care
because it's an iterative, experimental process. Without knowing the plant, numerical
approximation can't be done anyway. It would be possible to do a step response test
but that's totally unnecessary.

To explain the touchy nature of the controller gains, the system looks like a severely
underdamped second-order system at the start, when initial velocity is high. But the
sticktion kicks in hard when the velocity starts to drop. That is why the system is
unstable at Ki = 0.000002 and only slightly underdamped at Ki = 0.0000015. The system
is, technically, non-linear. The linear portion covers the initial response at high
velocity. The non-linear portion covers the settling time at low velocity. The linear
portion needs small gains to be stable. The non-linear portion needs high gain to reach
steady-state. A different control scheme will probably work better (this one is not LTI),
but actually measuring the plant and sticktion would take way more effort than the project
actually needs. It wasn't really the point, either, but having these approximations will
definitely inform my control system design from here on out.
